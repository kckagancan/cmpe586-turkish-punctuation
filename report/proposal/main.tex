\begin{abstract}
Punctuation restoration models may rely on spelling and capitalization cues
that are unreliable in informal writing. We will study Turkish case loss,
Turkish character folding, and internal transpositions using deterministic
perturbations. A published punctuation BERT will provide an external reference;
matched clean and augmented BERTurk training will isolate the augmentation
recipe. A case-loss removal arm will examine that component without
redistributing its dose. Development on validation data will precede a frozen
held-out evaluation of clean accuracy, noise robustness, and punctuation-class
behavior. The contribution will be an auditable experimental study, without
assuming an improvement or proposing a new architecture.
\end{abstract}

\section{Motivation and research questions}
Punctuation restoration assigns punctuation marks to text in which those marks
are absent. A model trained on consistently written text may encounter a
different surface form when users omit capital letters, replace Turkish
characters with Latin alternatives, or mistype letters. For a cased model,
these changes can remove useful boundary cues and alter subword segmentation.
Turkish also requires locale-sensitive casing: capital \texttt{I} maps to
dotless \texttt{\i}, while capital \texttt{\.{I}} maps to \texttt{i}.
A generic lowercasing rule would conflate distinct operations.

We will ask three linked questions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{RQ1: Sensitivity.} How does punctuation performance change with
noise family and severity, relative to each model's own clean performance?
\item \textbf{RQ2: Augmentation.} Does matched noise-augmented fine-tuning improve
performance on perturbed inputs, and what clean-text trade-off accompanies it?
\item \textbf{RQ3: Case-loss component.} How does removing case loss from
training augmentation affect clean, case-loss, and other noisy conditions?
\end{enumerate}
Our hypotheses are that corruption will generally reduce F1, augmentation will
reduce at least some of that loss, and retaining case loss will be most helpful
under case-loss evaluation. These are empirical expectations, not promised
outcomes: negative gains, non-monotonic severity effects, and clean-performance
costs will also be reported. We will not assign target F1 values in advance.

\section{Related work and intended contribution}
Kurt and \c{C}ay\i r~\cite{kurt2023} provide a Turkish punctuation restoration
study, released data, and pretrained checkpoints. Their release will supply
the task setting and an external reference model. Our controlled training
arms will instead start from the same saved BERTurk initialization~\cite{berturk},
so their difference can be interpreted under a shared experimental recipe.

Punctuation restoration and noise-aware training already intersect:
Sunkara et al.~\cite{sunkara2020} study punctuation and truecasing for medical
ASR and use augmentation to improve robustness to ASR errors. We will adapt
the general motivation to writing noise; we will preserve every original word
slot rather than importing ASR insertion/deletion alignment rules.
Karpukhin et al.~\cite{karpukhin2019} motivate synthetic-noise augmentation in
machine translation, but their findings do not establish its effect on Turkish
punctuation. N\'{a}plava et al.~\cite{naplava2021} study noise modeled from
user-generated errors. Our proposed perturbations will be controlled synthetic
stress conditions, without claiming that their frequencies represent Turkish
users.

The contribution will be a Turkish-focused comparison with deterministic
word-level perturbations, matched clean/augmented training, a case-loss removal
arm, and transparent evaluation limits. We will not claim architectural novelty,
state-of-the-art performance, or the first combination of punctuation restoration
and noise augmentation.

\section{Data and task formulation}
We will use the train, validation, and test CSV splits released with
Kurt and \c{C}ay\i r's work~\cite{kurt2023}, recording the exact upstream revision
and file hashes. Training will update model parameters; validation will support
development and clean-checkpoint selection; the held-out test will be reserved
for the final frozen comparisons. We will retain the released split membership
and audit malformed records, label validity, lengths, and exact input overlaps.
Rows containing empty word slots will be quarantined as whole records with
their IDs and exclusion reasons recorded; targets will not be shifted to
repair them.

Each original word will receive one of four labels:
\texttt{O}, \texttt{PERIOD}, \texttt{COMMA}, or \texttt{QUESTION\_MARK},
describing punctuation immediately after it. For token classification,
supervision will be attached to the first subword only; continuation subwords,
special tokens, and padding will use ignored label \texttt{-100}. Changed text
will be tokenized again while original word IDs, order, counts, and targets
remain fixed. Any missing alignment or sequence exceeding the 512-token limit
will stop preparation rather than silently truncate examples.

Dataset licensing, exact split provenance, and document independence are
unresolved in the available project record. We will document these limits and
avoid redistributing raw data. An exact-input-overlap sensitivity analysis will
remove test inputs matching released train or validation inputs and rescore the
same predictions. This will address one observable overlap issue, not prove
independence or rule out prior exposure of the published model.

\section{Proposed method}
\subsection{Controlled perturbations}
Table~\ref{tab:noise} defines the four evaluation families.
Single-family mild, medium, and heavy budgets will select exactly
$\lfloor pN\rfloor$ eligible positions, where $N$ is the number of original
word positions and $p\in\{0.02,0.05,0.10\}$. Stable hash ranking will select
positions without labels or model predictions; insufficient eligibility will
raise an error. Seeds 586, 587, and 588 will give paired corruption realizations,
with nested position masks across severities.

\begin{table}[t]
\caption{Planned writing-noise operators. Punctuation targets will stay unchanged.}
\label{tab:noise}
\centering
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{0.18\linewidth}p{0.77\linewidth}@{}}
\toprule
Family & Operation and control \\
\midrule
Case loss & Turkish-aware lowercasing of selected words with a case change. \\
Turkish folding & Explicit case-preserving mapping of
\c{c}, \u{g}, \i, \"{o}, \c{s}, \"{u} to c, g, i, o, s, u and uppercase counterparts. \\
Internal swap & One adjacent unequal interior-letter swap in an eligible
alphabetic word of at least four letters; first and last letters preserved. \\
Combined & Reuse component plans; apply swap, case loss, then folding.
The union can exceed the single-family changed-word budget. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Full-case-loss and full-folding endpoints will be reported separately from the
budgeted grid. We will record realized component edits and final union coverage.
Equal component budgets will not be described as equal information loss, and
combined noise will not be interpreted as an equal-dose interaction experiment.

\subsection{Models and matched training}
A published Turkish punctuation BERT~\cite{kurt2023} will provide an external
reference with an uncontrolled training history. The primary controlled
comparison will examine the training recipe difference between two
BERTurk arms: \textbf{Clean}, using original inputs, and \textbf{Augmented},
replacing selected rows with noisy views. Both will use the same saved encoder
and freshly initialized four-label head, training seed 586, eligible records,
shuffled row order, optimizer settings, and update budget. All parameters will
be fine-tuned. The published reference will not be treated as an untrained
encoder or an initialization-matched baseline.

The fixed recipe will use three full epochs, AdamW with learning rate
$2\times10^{-5}$ and weight decay 0.01 excluding biases and LayerNorm,
a physical batch of eight with four-step accumulation, linear 10\% warmup and
decay, gradient clipping at 1.0, and FP32 execution on the audited Colab GPU
runtime. Cross-entropy will be unweighted over all four labels, including
\texttt{O}, and normalized by supervised word count across each accumulation
window. Three epochs will define a feasible matched budget, not a claim of
convergence or hyperparameter optimality.

For each augmented epoch, combined-medium word plans will be built on the
training split, then exactly half the rows (rounded down) selected by
label-independent hash ranking for noisy views. Rows will be replaced in place,
without appending examples. Plans and selected rows will refresh each epoch
using training-specific seeds. Some selected views may remain unchanged;
we will record this rather than force edits or resample. The row fraction will
not be reported as a changed-word rate.

Every arm will complete the same budget. Its checkpoint will be the completed
epoch with highest \emph{clean-validation punctuation macro-F1}, choosing the
earlier epoch on an exact tie. Noisy validation will diagnose robustness rather
than choose the checkpoint. If selected epochs differ, we will also report a
matched final-epoch sensitivity comparison. Shared exposure and updates will not imply equal
wall-clock time or FLOPs because tokenization lengths can differ.

\subsection{Case-loss removal ablation}
A third BERTurk training arm will omit case loss while retaining the same saved
initialization, row masks, ordering, remaining component plans, and budget.
The omitted dose will not be reassigned to folding or swaps. We will compare
it with the full augmentation arm on clean input and each medium-noise family
using corruption seed 586. This asks what changes when that component is removed
from this recipe. Since total corruption also decreases, it will not establish
an equal-dose ranking of component importance.

\section{Evaluation and analysis plan}
After validation-based development, we will freeze model identities, selected
checkpoints, input hashes, noise plans, cohorts, and metric definitions before
project test scoring. The intended main matrix has 39 conditions per main
model: one clean condition, four families at three severities and three seeds,
and two full-loss endpoints. Three main models therefore require 117 forward
conditions. Five ablation conditions will give 122 in total; existing matched
reference predictions will be reused. Primary and overlap-filtered cohorts
will be scored from these same predictions, without re-noising or additional
forward passes. This detailed plan is reconstructed here, not claimed as an
original pre-project commitment.

The primary metric will be
\[
 F_{\mathrm{macro}}=\frac{1}{3}
 \left(F_{1,\mathrm{PERIOD}}+F_{1,\mathrm{COMMA}}
       +F_{1,\mathrm{QUESTION\_MARK}}\right).
\]
The \texttt{O} class will be omitted only from the punctuation average:
its positions will still contribute false positives and false negatives.
We will report per-class precision, recall, F1, supports, punctuation
micro-averages, and four-class confusion matrices. Robustness loss will compare
each model with its own clean score. Augmentation gains will be computed on
paired inputs for each corruption seed, then summarized by mean and sample
standard deviation. These seeds will measure corruption variation for fixed
models, not training variation, statistical significance, or equivalence.

We will inspect edited words, unchanged words in edited contexts, and untouched
rows separately, alongside deterministic error samples retained locally.
These diagnostics will describe where errors occur without attributing them
to a proven attention mechanism. We will publish the full planned condition
matrix, including unfavorable contrasts, without pooling unrelated conditions
into a single headline score. Test results will not trigger checkpoint or
recipe selection; later test-informed changes will be exploratory and need
genuinely unseen data for strong independent confirmation.

\section{Feasibility, risks, and deliverables}
The core scope will fit one student project: one encoder family, three matched
fine-tunings, one training seed, and a bounded evaluation matrix.
A short technical calibration will check memory and throughput before any
authorized long run. Runtime and source checks will fail closed on mismatch.
If interrupted, runs will resume only from a compatible recorded checkpoint;
incompatible changes will receive new identities rather than overwrite runs.
Single-seed uncertainty and synthetic-only evaluation will remain explicit
limitations. We will also avoid presenting preserved targets as proof that
every corrupted string has a unique linguistic reading.
